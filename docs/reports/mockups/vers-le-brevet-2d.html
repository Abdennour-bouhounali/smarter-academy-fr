Vers le brevet, in 2D

Three new looks, each built on what research and the apps teenagers use every day say about holding the attention of a 14-year-old. Every screen is live: press 12, 6 or 18. Sound is off until you turn it on.

1. Arcade

A night screen with loud colour and chunky keys you want to press, in the family of the mobile games this age plays.

Where it comes from

Mobile game interfaces: dark ground, thick dark edges, one bright main button.

Why it should hold him

It looks like something he chose to open, not like school. The maths is the brightest thing on a quiet ground.

Its risk

The furthest from the exam paper, and a dark screen is weaker on a classroom projector.

2. Affiche

One flat colour fills the screen and changes with his answer. Giant type, no boxes.

Where it comes from

Year-in-review stories and short-video apps: one idea per screen, said very big.

Why it should hold him

The whole screen answers him. Red when cakes are left, green when it is right: nobody has to read a verdict.

Its risk

Colour alone must never carry the verdict, so the sentence stays. Long statements leave less room for giant type.

3. Tactile

A light screen of soft raised tiles, with the key words as stickers.

Where it comes from

The soft-depth style of current learning apps: rounded, raised, friendly.

Why it should hold him

The calmest of the three and the easiest to read for a long exercise. It is also the best on a projector.

Its risk

The closest to « childish » for a 14-year-old if the stickers and curves are pushed any further.

My choice, for you to overrule

Arcade for the student, with Affiche's full-screen colour change kept for the right answer and the end of a step. Tactile becomes the teacher's board version, since a light screen projects better.
